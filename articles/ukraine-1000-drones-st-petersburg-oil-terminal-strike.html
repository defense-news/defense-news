Ukraine Launches More Than 1,000 Drones at Russia, Setting St. Petersburg Oil Terminal Ablaze

Michael ReynoldsOct 19, 2026 12:39 UTC 3 min readRegion: Europe

Ukrainian drones set an oil export terminal and a depot ablaze in St. Petersburg on Sunday as Kyiv launched more than 1,000 drones at Russia in one of the largest aerial attacks of the war, Russian authorities and company officials said.

The fires at the Petersburg Oil Terminal and the city's Ruchi oil depot marked a striking blow against Russian energy infrastructure in Vladimir Putin's home city, hundreds of kilometers from the front lines. Regional Governor Alexander Beglov said one person was killed and five others were wounded in the attack. A logistics center belonging to Russian online retailer Ozon and a data center operated by Russian tech giant VK were also hit, both companies confirmed.

Russia's Defense Ministry said its air defenses had destroyed 1,015 Ukrainian drones over 15 Russian regions, the annexed Ukrainian peninsula of Crimea and the Black Sea. Moscow's mayor reported intercepting dozens of drones around the capital, and aviation authorities temporarily restricted operations at two Moscow airports and St. Petersburg's Pulkovo airport. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told the Tass news agency that Kyiv was "resisting" Moscow's invasion but could not "change the current course of events."

The attack came as tensions between Ukraine and Washington deepened. President Donald Trump announced Oct. 11 that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an "energy ceasefire," but offered no details. Kyiv initially said it was observing the pause, then said Thursday it had resumed strikes on Russian oil facilities after a three-day halt. Trump last week called Ukraine's strikes on Russian oil refineries a "disgrace," and on Friday night signed an executive order waiving planned restrictions, citing U.S. national interests — a move that put further U.S. sanctions against Moscow in doubt.

A ceasefire that never held

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy wrote on X Sunday that Russia had attacked Ukraine's energy, oil and gas sectors 76 times in the week since Trump's announcement. Energy facilities in four Ukrainian regions — Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr, Khmelnytskyi and Odesa — were struck overnight, leaving tens of thousands of families without power, he said, accusing Moscow of trying to cause a blackout ahead of winter.

"America should see that Russia is not taking any reciprocal steps in response to sanctions relief or agreements," Zelenskyy wrote. "On the contrary, they take every signal of an opportunity to make money as proof that their bet on war is paying off and they can keep fighting."

Russia continued its own strikes on Ukraine. In Zaporizhzhia, a woman was killed and a 6-year-old girl was among at least two others wounded in two Russian attacks Sunday, local officials said. Overnight on Oct. 18–19, Kyiv was hit by multiple waves of ballistic missiles that damaged a medical center, a 25-story residential building and the Chernihivska metro station, killing at least one person, Ukrainian officials said.

Speaking on NBC's "Meet the Press" on Sunday, Zelenskyy said waiving sanctions would strengthen Putin's war effort. He also said U.S.-Ukraine intelligence sharing had resumed after being "blocked for some period of time," and added that while he trusted Trump, siding with Moscow would be a tragedy and a "historic mistake" for Washington.

Why it matters

The St. Petersburg raid is a measure of how completely long-range drones have rewritten the geography of the war. An attack of this scale — against petroleum terminals, a retail logistics hub and a tech company's data center in Russia's second city — shows Ukraine can now impose visible, economically meaningful costs far inside Russia with unmanned systems built domestically at a fraction of the cost of traditional airpower.

It also sharpens the contradiction at the heart of Trump's diplomacy. The administration wants lower oil prices ahead of the midterms and is pressing Kyiv to stop hitting Russian refineries, while exempting Russian diesel imports from sanctions. Zelenskyy's answer — that he has spoken with U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner about next steps and will keep striking — is that a one-sided ceasefire is no ceasefire at all. Watch whether the St. Petersburg fires push Washington to settle its sanctions decision, and whether Moscow retaliates against Ukrainian energy as winter sets in. The winter blackout contest is now the center of gravity for both sides.
